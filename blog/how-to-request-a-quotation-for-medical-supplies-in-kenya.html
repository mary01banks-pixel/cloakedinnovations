How to Request a Quotation for Medical Supplies in Kenya

A complete request gets a faster and more accurate quotation. Here is what to put in it, how to describe the products, and how public entities in Kenya buy.

By the Cloaked Innovations team. Published 10 November 2026. 5 minute read.

Key takeaways

  • A good request names the items, specifications, quantities and units, delivery location and date, and the documents you need.
  • Public entities in Kenya buy under the Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act, 2015, and its 2020 Regulations include requests for quotations as a method. [1]
  • Specifications prevent substitutions. Describe the use and name the standard, size and pack size.
  • We usually respond within 5 to 60 minutes during business hours, and a quotation does not commit you to buy.

When to ask for a quotation

A request for quotation is the usual route when you know what you need and want a price, availability and delivery terms. Kenya's 2020 procurement regulations include requests for quotations and low value procurement among the methods public entities can use. [1] Private buyers, schools, NGOs and clinics follow the same practice: describe the need clearly and ask for a written offer. Ask early, because lead times for equipment and less common items can be longer than for everyday consumables.

Put your full list in one request, and say whether you want one quotation for everything or separate quotations for each category. If you need a particular format, or documents alongside the quotation, say so in the first message.

Write specifications that cannot be misread

The most common cause of a wrong quotation is a vague line such as "gloves" or "masks". Say what the product is for, then add material, size, sterility, the standard you want and the pieces per pack. Our guides show what to specify for each category:

  • Disposable gloves and face masks
  • Wound care supplies and syringes and needles
  • Catheters and tubing and diagnostic equipment
  • Waste management supplies

If you do not know the exact specification, describe how the product will be used and tell us. We can confirm options before we quote. For quantities, use daily use x days of cover x a buffer, as explained in our PPE guide.

If you are a public entity

Public procurement in Kenya is governed by the Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Act, 2015 and the 2020 Regulations. [1] The Act is overseen by bodies including the Public Procurement Regulatory Authority, and it covers procedures, evaluation and disputes. [3] Entities issue their own request documents. One Kenyan university request, for instance, stated a quotation validity, required prices in Kenya shillings and said the lowest evaluated quotation would be recommended. [2]

If you are answering such a request, send us the request document, reference number, closing date and any required documents so we can respond to its requirements. Once offers arrive, our guide to comparing supplier offers explains how to evaluate them.

A request template

Template: "We are [organisation] in [town]. Please quote for: [item, specification, size, standard, quantity and unit]. Delivery to [location] by [date]. We need [documents]. Reference number: [number, if any]. This is a [one off or recurring] order. Contact: [name and phone]."

Here is the same template filled in: "We are a school clinic in Kisumu. Please quote for 20 boxes of powder free nitrile examination gloves, size M, 100 per box, and 10 boxes of medical face masks with ear loops. Delivery to our clinic by the end of the month. Please include expiry dates. We expect to reorder every term."

How to send it

Use the contact form, write to info@cloakedinnovations.co.ke with attachments, or call 0796 968 241 during business hours. Our quotation guide explains what happens next, and the products page lists examples of what we supply. Products there are examples, not a complete list, so ask for anything else you need. We review your requirements, confirm availability, contact you if anything is unclear, and then send the quotation. Registration of health products in Kenya is governed by the Pharmacy and Poisons Board rules, so tell us if you need registration details with the quotation. [4]
