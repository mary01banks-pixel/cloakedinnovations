Catheters and Tubing: What to Specify Before You Request a Quote

IV cannulas, urinary catheters and feeding tubes each have their own sizing system and connector. Getting the details right in your request prevents costly mismatches.

By the Cloaked Innovations team. Published 7 November 2026. 5 minute read.

Key takeaways

  • IV cannula gauge runs backwards: a higher number means a thinner cannula. Colour codes follow ISO 10555 Part 5. [1]
  • Urinary catheters are sized in French. Guidance is to choose the smallest size that drains adequately, with a closed drainage system. [4][5]
  • Feeding tubes, syringes and giving sets must have matching connectors. ENFit follows ISO 80369 Part 3. [6][7]
  • Always state size, length, material, sterility, connector and pack size, and ask for documents.

Three product families

"Tubing and catheters" covers products that are bought and used very differently. Clinical staff choose the device for each patient, and procurement sets the range and writes a clear specification.

ProductState in your request
IV cannulasGauge, length, with or without wings and port, safety feature, sterile single use, pieces per box
Foley (urinary) cathetersFrench size, number of ways, balloon volume, material, adult or paediatric length, sterile, drainage bag if needed
Feeding tubesFrench size, length, material, connector type (for example ENFit), sterile

IV cannulas: gauge and range

Gauge is an inverse scale, so a 14G cannula is wider than a 22G. Manufacturers colour code the hub by size according to ISO 10555 Part 5. [1] The Red Cross procurement catalogue, for example, lists a 20G as pink with an average flow of about 61 ml a minute and a 22G as blue at about 36 ml a minute. [2] Flow figures differ between manufacturers, so read each data sheet.

Facilities rarely need a single gauge. One supply guide suggests treating 22G and 20G as high volume general stock and carrying 18G and 16G at a lower level, which your clinical team should confirm for your patients. [3] Write the range and a quantity per gauge into your request.

Urinary catheters: size and closed drainage

Foley catheters are sized in French (Fr), where a larger number means a wider catheter. [4] Clinical guidance is to use the smallest size that allows adequate drainage, because a larger catheter raises the risk of urethral trauma. [4][5] The most important risk factor for catheter associated urinary tract infection is prolonged use, and guidance calls for aseptic insertion and a drainage system that stays closed and sterile. [4][5]

For buyers, that means ordering a spread of sizes in sterile packs, with sterile closed drainage bags, and checking that packaging is intact on delivery. French size describes diameter, not length, so state the length you need as well. [9] Sterile gloves are also needed for insertion, so see our guide to choosing disposable gloves.

Feeding tubes: connectors

Small bore connectors that look alike have caused wrong route errors, where a feed is connected to an intravenous line. To prevent this, ISO 80369 Part 3 defines an enteral only connector called ENFit, approved worldwide in 2015 and published in 2016. [6] The connector fits only enteral devices. [7]

The practical risk for buyers is mixing connector types. Feeding tubes, syringes and giving sets must be compatible, and during the transition to ENFit some facilities needed transition connectors to join older tubes to ENFit syringes. [7] Ask which connector each product has, and order tubes, syringes and sets as a matched set.

Quantities and requesting a quotation

Estimate with procedures per day x days of cover x buffer. As an illustration with made up numbers, 40 cannulations a day for 30 days with a 20 percent buffer is 40 x 30 x 1.2 = 1,440 cannulas, to be split across your gauge range. Registration in Kenya is governed by the Pharmacy and Poisons (Registration of Health Products and Technologies) Rules, 2022, so ask for the manufacturer, registration status where it applies, expiry dates and test documents. [8]

Send your list through our contact page. IV cannulas, Foley catheters and feeding tubes are examples of what we supply on the products page, and our quotation guide explains what to include. You may also want our guide to syringes and needles.

Example request: "Please quote for sterile IV cannulas with ISO colour coding: 20G (40 boxes), 22G (30 boxes) and 18G (10 boxes), 50 per box. Also Foley catheters, two way, 14 Fr and 16 Fr, 200 each, with closed drainage bags. Please include documents and expiry dates. Delivery to Kisumu."
