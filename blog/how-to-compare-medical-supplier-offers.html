How Procurement Teams Can Compare Supplier Offers

The cheapest quotation is not always the best offer. Here is a simple method for comparing medical supply quotations fairly, defensibly and without missing the details that cost money later.

By the Cloaked Innovations team. Published 10 November 2026. 5 minute read.

Key takeaways

  • Put every offer into the same format first: same specification, unit, quantity and delivery terms.
  • Check that each offer meets your specification and document requirements before comparing prices. Kenyan public procurement rules separate technical and financial evaluation. [1]
  • Convert every price to a cost per piece or cost per use, then add delivery, shelf life and support.
  • Apply the criteria you set in writing. Auditors have flagged quotations assessed as responsive when they failed the stated criteria. [3]

Make offers comparable first

Offers rarely arrive in the same shape. One supplier quotes a box of 100, another a box of 50, a third a carton, and each may include or exclude delivery. Before you judge price, put every offer into one comparison sheet with the same columns: item and specification, unit and pieces per unit, unit price, cost per piece, delivery cost, documents supplied, shelf life and quotation validity.

Here is an illustration with made up numbers. Supplier A quotes examination gloves at KES 600 for a box of 100, which is KES 6.00 a glove. Supplier B quotes KES 400 for a box of 50, which is KES 8.00 a glove. B looks cheaper per box but costs a third more per glove. Our gloves guide shows how to write the specification so both suppliers quote the same product.

Check compliance, then compare price

The Public Procurement and Asset Disposal Regulations, 2020 set out separate steps for technical evaluation, financial evaluation and post qualification. [1] A sensible order for any buyer follows the same logic. First, check that each offer meets your specification and document requirements. Then compare prices only among the offers that qualify.

Public requests for quotations often recommend the lowest evaluated quotation, not simply the lowest price. One Kenyan university request, for example, held quotations valid for seven days, required prices in Kenya shillings and recommended the lowest evaluated quotation for award. [2] Lowest evaluated means the lowest price among offers that meet every requirement.

A checklist for every offer

  • Specification match: material, size, sterility, standard and pack size as written in your request
  • Documents: manufacturer, batch and expiry information, test reports and the registration status of the product in Kenya where it applies [4]
  • Availability and lead time: stock on hand or on order, and the delivery date
  • Shelf life: remaining shelf life on delivery
  • Delivery terms: cost, location and who handles damage or shortages
  • Validity and payment terms: how long the price holds and when payment is due
  • Support: replacement of wrong or damaged items, and for equipment, warranty and service [5]

Weight the criteria that matter most to you. For consumables, a matching specification and adequate shelf life often matter more than a small price difference.

Red flags

Treat these as reasons to ask questions before you accept an offer:

  • A price far below every other offer, with no explanation
  • Missing or refused documents, or no batch and expiry details
  • Vague specifications, or substitutes offered without notice
  • No physical address, or contact only through social media
  • Pressure to decide before you have checked the documents

WHO estimates that at least 1 in 10 medicines in low and middle income countries is substandard or falsified, and notes that these products are often sold online or in informal markets. [6] The estimate concerns medicines, but the lesson for any buyer is to prefer suppliers you can verify and to report suspicious products to the national regulator.

Apply your criteria and keep records

A Kenyan Auditor General report found quotations assessed as responsive even though they failed the criteria in the tender documents, and also noted the lack of individual evaluator score sheets that the 2020 Regulations require. [3] Public or private, the lesson is the same. Write your criteria before you open the offers, score each offer against them, and keep the sheet with your reasons for the award.

Frequently asked questions

The lowest price is simply the smallest number quoted. The lowest evaluated price is the smallest price among offers that meet every requirement in your specification and documents.

Convert every price to a cost per piece by dividing the price by the number of pieces in the unit. For consumables with different usage rates, compare cost per use.

Ask for the manufacturer, batch and expiry details, the standards the product meets with supporting reports, storage instructions and its registration status in Kenya where it applies.

Your procurement policy decides this. One Kenyan university request held quotations valid for seven days, so state the validity you need in your request.

Note: this guide supports purchasing decisions. It is not clinical or infection control advice. Follow your facility's infection prevention and control policy and current Ministry of Health guidance.
